Long before stadium-selling tours filled arenas in London, New York, and Paris, Nigerian sound was forged in the studios of Surulere and Festac Town. Today, Afrobeats is no longer just a genre of music—it is a macroeconomic export engine reshaping global pop culture and attracting institutional capital.

### Beyond the Streaming Numbers

While billions of global streams on Spotify and Apple Music grab headlines, the true economic revolution lies in the ancillary industries built around the movement. From fashion designers dressing global superstars to sync licensing in Hollywood blockbusters and international brand partnerships, Afrobeats has unlocked soft power on an unprecedented scale.

### The Business of Creativity

Nigerian creative entrepreneurs are building vertically integrated enterprises. Record labels, talent incubators, and experiential production companies are professionalizing the creative value chain. Investors who once treated the creative arts as speculative are now recognizing intellectual property as a defensible, high-yielding asset class.

As the world tunes in, the economic returns are flowing back into local recording studios, live event production, and creative academies across Lagos and Abuja, proving that culture is one of Nigeria's most lucrative renewable resources.
